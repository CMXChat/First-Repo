## Purpose

This note preserves two ideas that should remain visible on `/doc/` without adding another top-level section or turning the page into a technical manual:

1. Continuum can eventually carry specific approved work forward on a person's behalf when that person cannot participate directly.
2. A long-running Goal can eventually expose a limitation in Continuum itself and lead to a governed architecture change.

These ideas sit inside the existing Continuity, Goals, Capability and Build teaching. They do not change current LIVE/LAB/NEXT/LATER truth.

## Continuity of authorized intent

Afterlife is more than a timer concept. The timer is the current LIVE trigger foundation. The longer-term idea is that a person can prepare context, instructions, people, priorities, limits and authority while they are available, then allow selected work to continue later under a published continuity policy.

Public wording should stay plain:

> **Specific work can continue on your behalf when you cannot take part directly.**

Future conceptual flow:

`PREPARED INTENT → CURRENT STATE → CONTINUITY POLICY → AUTHORIZED ACTION → WAIT / REPLY → CONTINUE → AUDIT`

Examples of unavailability can include:

- sleep or travel;
- a meeting or loss of connectivity;
- hospitalization;
- extended incapacity;
- long-term continuity after death.

The applicable policy decides what happens in each case.

The page should explain that Future Runtime may eventually:

- check Current State;
- identify the people selected in advance;
- use only the information approved for the continuity task;
- contact people through approved Connections;
- release selected information;
- wait for replies;
- handle the next permitted step;
- escalate when the policy requires it;
- keep a durable Audit trail.

Core rule:

> **Your absence never expands the authority already published.**

Silence or absence may change State or make an existing fallback policy eligible. It never creates new permission.

## Across-time teaching

The existing Across Time progression should make the continuity idea easy to understand:

`WITH YOU → FOR YOU → WHEN YOU ARE AWAY → IF YOU CANNOT RESPOND`

The last stage should communicate:

> **Carry approved intent forward**

Continuum can use the people, information, priorities and fallback rules prepared beforehand when direct approval is unavailable.

This language should remain grounded. Avoid supernatural, digital-clone or immortality framing. The product idea is durable context plus authorized agency across time.

## Goals during unavailability

A future Goal can keep moving during a person's unavailability when its published continuity policy allows that path.

The Goal keeps the same:

- success criteria;
- hard constraints;
- budgets and limits;
- authority;
- stop conditions.

Continuity changes who or what may provide the next permitted decision or Action. It does not silently rewrite the Goal.

## Architecture evolution

Continuum's adaptability goes deeper than adding an API or MCP server.

A Goal may eventually expose a recurring structural limitation in Continuum itself. Examples include a missing data model, domain service, Runtime primitive, capability mapping or workflow concept.

Public conceptual flow:

`GOAL BLOCKED → GAP → CHANGE PLAN → CODE + TESTS → SIMULATE → AUTHORIZE → RELEASE → MEASURE`

The public page should explain this once, inside the existing Build material.

Example:

A long-running hiring Goal keeps forcing interview stages into generic notes. Continuum could eventually propose a typed interview-stage model, database migration, service changes and tests, then measure whether the released change makes that Goal more reliable.

Architecture evolution remains governed engineering work. It uses:

- versioning;
- migration checks;
- tests;
- simulation/staging where applicable;
- release authority;
- Audit/provenance;
- rollback;
- outcome measurement.

Permission stays governed separately from architecture capability.

## Information quality from the latest clarity pass

The latest `/doc/` pass also added an important information-quality distinction that should remain:

`Observation / Claim / Derived conclusion / Current State`

Continuum should keep source, time, freshness and conflicts attached instead of treating every incoming statement as equivalent truth.

Example:

`Client says payment will arrive Friday` remains a Claim. A verified account event showing that the payment posted can update Current State.

This supports better reasoning and safer continuity decisions because evidence quality remains visible.

## Public writing guardrails

Keep the language human and restrained.

- no ellipses;
- no em dashes;
- avoid `it's not X, it's Y` framing;
- avoid repetitive futuristic claims;
- avoid describing Continuum as conscious, alive or a digital copy of a person;
- say what it can eventually do in concrete operational terms;
- preserve LIVE/LAB/NEXT/LATER labels;
- keep capability and permission separate;
- keep current implementation truth separate from future architecture.

The goal is simple: a first-time reader should understand that Continuum can eventually preserve enough approved context, intent and authority to keep specific work moving across a person's absence, while remaining inspectable and governed.
