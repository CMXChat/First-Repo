The whole idea

Spaces in one minute

A person, family, pair, business partnership, adviser relationship, or team can open one current Brief built from the information that belongs there. The Space keeps the context behind that Brief so the next conversation does not start from zero.

Most digital tools understand one slice of your life. A calendar knows time, email holds messages, task managers track assignments, and a new AI chat usually starts with whatever you explain again.

Spaces organizes those slices around the person or group who needs them. A Family Space can bring together approved calendars, chores, shopping, weather, and household decisions while each record keeps a clear owner and permission scope.

The Brief shows what changed, what matters now, what needs a decision, and what can wait. Conversation stays available across the whole Space or from a focused section such as a weather card, cash sheet, calendar, or project board.

How the context builds

A Space stays current through connected accounts, direct input, and corrections

Most of the work should happen after the user chooses what to connect and which Space can use it. Spaces asks a short question only when the answer could change the next step.

Connected accounts

Calendar, email, files, project tools, finance, music, and other approved services can supply records within a clear read, write, sync, and Space scope.

Direct input

A note, uploaded document, voice update, form, import, or section-level conversation can add context through a short, focused path.

Focused questions

The AI can ask one clear question when a missing answer would change a recommendation, deadline, permission, or decision.

Corrections

A direct user correction replaces a weaker guess, keeps the earlier record in revision history, and updates the next Brief.

Complex human coordination

Spaces can help people coordinate while keeping private context separate

The most important shared use cases involve context that belongs to more than one person and still requires careful privacy, consent, and interpretation.

Couples

Shared plans with separate private context

A Relationship Space can hold plans, rituals, decisions, promises, shared goals, and approved memories while each person retains a separate Personal Space.

  • Compare schedules before suggesting time together
  • Track an agreement after both people approve it
  • Summarize stated concerns and leave the decision to the people involved
  • Keep private information out of the shared view

Families

A household operating layer

A Family Space can organize approved calendar events, care, school, meals, appointments, travel, shopping, chores, and household decisions with age-appropriate permissions. Each person keeps a private profile, and private events can contribute a busy block while their title and notes stay in the owner’s profile.

  • Show each person only what they need
  • Coordinate approved events, rides, and availability across several calendars
  • Give chores and shopping items a clear owner and status
  • Preserve family memories and important dates
  • Escalate a missed responsibility to the right adult

Teams and organizations

One shared truth with role-specific access

A Team Space can hold the project state, responsibilities, handoffs, procedures, risks, decisions, and approved financial signals. Leadership may see broader operating context while each member sees relevant work and the information needed to complete it.

The investment case

Why this is a credible product investment

Established products already prove that people want help with projects, schedules, family coordination, finances, and connected tools. The opportunity is to bring those needs into one governed daily product that carries context forward and keeps private and shared records in the right place.

PROTECTION SEQUENCE

Protect the implementation before the next technical reveal

  1. 1

    Record the invention now. Name each inventor, save dated diagrams and prototypes, explain the technical flow, and log every public disclosure.

  2. 2

    Search the actual mechanism. Ask patent counsel to examine scoped context, provenance, private and shared derivation, revision history, ranking, and approval controls against prior art.

  3. 3

    File only when the detail supports it. If counsel finds a viable invention, a complete provisional can establish a United States filing date. It is not examined and normally needs a related nonprovisional within 12 months.

  4. 4

    Layer the protection. Keep internal ranking, evaluation, and connector methods confidential; clear the final brand for trademark; preserve authorship records for code and original visual work.

COMMERCIAL SEQUENCE

Sell one shared decision loop before funding every scenario

RECOMMENDED FIRST PAID WEDGE Remote business partners and small teams

They have an identifiable buyer, recurring shared decisions, existing work data, and less regulated risk than beginning with financial advice or money movement.

  • Build: secure identity, a reliable daily Brief, calendar and project connections, direct corrections, permissions, and approved actions.
  • Prove: activation, weekly return, Brief to action rate, shared invitations, correction use, permission comprehension, and paid retention.
  • Charge: start with a subscription for each shared Space or team, then add household and professional client plans after the trust controls are proven.
  • Expand: add integration and platform partnerships after customers rely on the core loop and can explain the outcome they pay for.

Questions

Common questions about Spaces

Is Spaces an AI model?

Spaces is the product around the model. It keeps the contexts, permissions, memory, sources, goals, tools, approvals, and history that let a chosen model work inside clear limits.

Why is the product organized around Spaces?

Each Space keeps one part of life coherent. Remote partners can share company operations, a family can coordinate household plans, and an accountant and client can review approved financial records without pulling unrelated context into those Spaces.

How does a Space get its information?

Information can come from connected accounts, uploaded files, notes, voice updates, forms, imports, conversations, public sources, and short questions from the AI. Every source needs a purpose, permission, and Space.

Can users correct what a Space remembers?

That is part of the planned Memory & Data settings. Users should be able to inspect sources, correct records, move information between Spaces, remove interpretations, change connector permissions, export data, delete records, and pause future learning.

How does the Brief decide what appears first?

The current demo combines a user’s stated priorities with time, impact, risk, commitments, and recent changes. Engagement can help tune presentation over time, but important facts remain visible even when they are less interesting.

What happens when I ask AI from a section?

The current demo prepares a prompt with the selected Space and section, then shows the context handoff. A production account would add authorized records, source labels, permission scope, and approved tools before sending the request to a model.

Can different AI models use the same Space?

Yes. The Space is designed to keep its goals, memory, permissions, and history while the user or system selects a suitable model for the task.

What data does the current demo use?

The public demo uses sourced public information and clearly fictional private-looking records. Real private data requires authentication, protected storage, server-side permissions, limited connector scopes, and revocable access.

Can Spaces help with family, relationship, or team decisions?

Spaces can organize what people stated, expose schedule or responsibility conflicts, track approved agreements, and prepare practical options. People remain responsible for the relationship and the decision.

What makes the alarm, music, and voice experience useful?

The routine can use the same approved context as the Brief, so music, voice, reminders, and spoken check-ins begin from the current Space with the needed context already available.
