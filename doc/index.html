What Spaces is

A workspace that carries context forward

Spaces gives an individual or group one continuing place where approved information can stay connected to the people, goals, decisions, and permissions that give it meaning.

Your calendar knows when something happens. Email knows what was said. A project tool knows what was assigned. A finance app knows what moved. An AI conversation knows what you happened to explain in that thread. The useful picture is usually spread across all of them.

A Space brings the relevant parts together for one purpose. A Personal Space can hold private goals, routines, money, schedule, and memory. A Family Space can use approved calendars, chores, shopping, weather, and household plans. A Business Space can carry projects, decisions, local time, shared concerns, and the operating context that both partners need.

The product uses that continuing context to build a Brief, answer questions from the right starting point, surface conflicts or changes, recommend a useful next step, and prepare actions that remain subject to the permissions of that Space.

The durable layer

The AI can change while the Space keeps its context

Spaces keeps permissions, sources, memory, goals, corrections, tools, approvals, and history outside the model. A default model can power the experience today, another model can handle a different task tomorrow, and the Space still knows what belongs there.

The daily front door

The Brief is the current view of a Space

The Brief is one of the main outputs of Spaces. It gives the reader a useful current picture without pretending that the entire product is a briefing app.

01

Changes

Show what is different since the last useful view

New events, messages, money movement, project changes, weather, progress, deadlines, and other approved updates can surface when they affect the current Space.

02

Attention

Separate what matters now from what can wait

The Brief can rank by time, impact, risk, stated priorities, commitments, and unresolved decisions while keeping the source visible.

03

Conversation

Ask from the part of the Brief you are already looking at

A question from a calendar, finance card, project section, weather view, or goal can begin with that approved context already attached.

04

Next step

Move from information to a prepared decision or action

Spaces can explain why something deserves attention, ask for missing information, prepare an action, and record the outcome after the user confirms what happened.

Different Spaces can produce different kinds of Briefs from the same product foundation.

A personal morning view, household plan, partner operating Brief, accountant review, training update, or team handoff can each emphasize different information while using the same underlying rules for ownership, permissions, memory, goals, and source history.

Context and connections

A Space becomes useful by learning from approved sources and direct corrections

The user decides what can enter a Space. Once that boundary is set, the product can do more of the organizing work without repeatedly asking the person to explain the same background.

Connected accounts

Calendar, email, files, project tools, finance, music, and other services can supply records within a defined read, write, sync, and Space scope.

Direct input

A note, document, voice update, form, import, or conversation can add information through a clear path that keeps the source attached.

Focused questions

The AI can ask one specific question when a missing answer would materially change a recommendation, deadline, permission, or decision.

Corrections

A user correction can replace a weaker assumption, preserve revision history, and update the next Brief without losing where the earlier interpretation came from.

Product boundary

The interface can explain privacy, but real protection has to exist behind it

The public overview and demo contain product explanation plus fictional examples. A production Spaces account requires authenticated sessions, server-side authorization, protected storage, connector scopes, encryption, audit history, revocable access, and clear controls for each shared Space.

Questions

Common questions about Spaces

Is Spaces an AI model?

Spaces is the product layer that gives an AI model usable context. It keeps the Space, permissions, memory, sources, goals, tools, approvals, and history that allow a selected model to work from a controlled foundation.

Is the Brief the whole product?

No. The Brief is a main way to see the current state of a Space. The wider product also includes the Space itself, connected information, memory, goals, conversations, documents, projects, settings, permissions, corrections, and approved actions.

Why organize the product around Spaces?

Different parts of life need different people, records, goals, and boundaries. A Family Space can coordinate a household, a Business Space can carry company operations, and an Accountant Space can expose approved financial records without pulling unrelated personal context into the engagement.

How does a Space get information?

Information can come from connected accounts, files, notes, voice updates, forms, imports, conversations, public sources, and focused questions. Each source needs a clear purpose, permission, and Space.

Can users correct what a Space remembers?

That is part of the planned Memory and Data controls. Users should be able to inspect sources, correct records, move information between Spaces, remove interpretations, change connector permissions, export data, delete records, and pause future learning.

How does the Brief decide what appears first?

The product can combine stated priorities with time, impact, risk, commitments, unresolved decisions, and recent changes. The ranking logic should remain testable and important information should stay visible even when it is less engaging.

What happens when I ask AI from a section?

The current demo shows the intended context handoff. A production account would attach the selected Space, section, authorized records, source labels, permission scope, and approved tools before the request is sent to a model.

Can different AI models use the same Space?

Yes. The Space is designed to keep its goals, memory, permissions, sources, and history while the user or product chooses a suitable model for the task.

What data does the current demo use?

The public demo uses sourced public information and clearly fictional private-looking records. Real private data requires authenticated accounts, protected storage, server-side permissions, limited connector scopes, revocable access, and audit history.
